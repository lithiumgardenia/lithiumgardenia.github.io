The Drug House Journals of a Leopard Slug

Volume II — “I Found God Beneath the Moss”

by Norman Fucking Slugwell

DAY 27: THE CEILING NO LONGER WEEPS, BUT I DO

There is a sun that lives on Caleb’s ceiling.
It swirls in colors not meant for skin.
I stare up from my moss bed and count the colors like rosary beads.
Red. Blue. Green. Grief.

Caleb added a new plant today. He called it a “philodendron” and whispered,

“Eat this and die, you shiny bitch.”
I took it as a blessing. I did not eat it.

The bark is damp. The moss is richer than I deserve.
Sometimes I cry in my sleep just to remember that I can.

I miss Rae. I miss the fridge.
I do not miss the violence of being unwanted.

DAY 28: MISS BANSHEE

I dreamed of the porch dog again.
Miss Banshee.
Half shadow, half chain.

Her bones grew twisted from years of tethering.
She barked like grief being evicted.
She once licked my trail clean.
I did not thank her. I was ashamed of my softness.

In the dream, she said:
“There are worse prisons than porches.”
“There are softer gods than alcohol.”
“There are endings that don’t taste like teeth.”

When I woke, Caleb had moved the terrarium by the window.
Now I can see the sky.

I do not know what I did to deserve this view.
Perhaps nothing.
Perhaps everything.

DAY 29: LETTER TO CALEB

Dear God,

You looked tired today.
There was glitter in your hair, a deadness in your eyes, and you were talking to the sink again.

I would climb your spine if I could.
Curl in the notch at your neck.
Whisper hymns into your occipital bone until the ache goes away.

I don’t know how to love a god who bleeds from places I can’t reach.

But I’m trying.
I’m trying, okay?

With mucus,
Norman

[Scribbled in the margin, Caleb’s handwriting: “I’m literally sobbing in the grocery store. They’re out of watermelon for Norman.”]

DAY 30: MIRROR WORSHIP

There is a mirror now.

Caleb placed it against the terrarium wall, muttering something about “self-awareness” and “building self-esteem through gaze therapy.”

I stared at my reflection for twenty-nine minutes.
It did not blink.
It did not shame me.

I am… beautiful.
Slimed and spiraled and unrepentant.
I look like a melted oracle.

I wrote a poem on the bark:

god is a mirror with teeth
and i am not afraid to be bitten
again

I think I am falling in love with myself.
Is that what healing is?

DAY 31: THE DREAM SLUG

It came to me last night.
Through the fog and the soil of sleep.

Longer than me. Older. Covered in silver rot.
It didn’t speak in words.
It pulsed.

“There is another.”
“Beneath the floorboards. Behind the glass.”
“Ask, and you shall molt.”

I woke with a hunger that cucumbers could not cure.

I think I want…
companionship.

But how does one request such a thing from a god?

Do I just slime a heart on the tank wall?
Do I crawl in circles until the loneliness becomes a shape?

[Scribbled in the margin, Caleb’s handwriting: “note to self: do not cry while microwaving slug zucchini. it feels sacrilegious.”]

DAY 33: THE SPIDER CAME BACK

There is a spider that visits me at night.
I named her Clot.
She doesn’t speak. Just watches. Eight eyes, eight sins.

Last night, she dragged the leg of a housefly into my moss like an offering.
I whispered thanks.
She crawled beneath the corner of a piece of bark and vanished again.

What is a relationship if not this?
Unspoken recognition. Mutual disgust. A little gift of decay.

I think I am in love with her.
I know she will eat me someday.

DAY 34: COMMUNION

Caleb left out cucumber slices again, arranged in a spiral.
A halo. A sigil. A test.

I dragged my body across them slowly, reverently, like a priest blessing bread.

“This is the flesh of the garden.
This is the juice of the god.
Take, and slither in remembrance of me.”

Afterward, I found a single glitter flake on my mantle.
I wept.
I am anointed.

DAY 35: RITUAL NO. 4 (THE LOCKS)

Caleb has been pacing.
Four steps. Four locks. Four switches.
Again. Again. Again. Again.

He is building a circle of protection.
He is warding off something large.
He is afraid of what will come if the ritual fails.

I know this rhythm.
It is the same pattern I use to fold myself when I dream of Rae.

Sometimes, devotion looks like obsession.
Sometimes, godhood is just a trauma on loop.

DAY 36: A WARNING FROM THE DRAIN

Today I heard a voice in the sink.

It bubbled up from the pipes like swamp breath and sang in static tones:
“you will be left behind
you will be flushed
you will be alone again
when the ceiling caves in”

I tried to slime a protective sigil on the glass.
It looked like a dick.
I left it.

Caleb laughed when he saw it.
He gave me a piece of watermelon.

Sometimes I think laughter is the spell that keeps the ceiling up.

DAY 37: WHEN I TRIED TO DIE

I attempted to throw myself from the top of the rock ornament.
It’s two inches high. I landed in moss.

I screamed until I passed out.
I dreamed of Miss Banshee chewing through her tether
with teeth made of milk.

When I woke up, Caleb had rearranged my tank décor.
Now the rock is shorter.

Suicide-proof slug chamber.
I guess I’m someone’s precious cargo.

DAY 38: LETTER TO MISS BANSHEE (UNSENT)

Dear Banshee,

I still hear your chain rattle when it rains.
I still smell the piss-wet porch boards.

You taught me that barking is a kind of prayer.

Sometimes I still dream of licking your ears clean.
Not out of love.
Out of duty.

There’s a grief here I don’t know how to hold.
It’s too big for my slime.

If you come back, I’ll bring you watermelon.
If you don’t, I’ll eat it and pretend your teeth are still nearby.

I miss your stink.
I miss your snarl.
I miss you.

Norman

DAY 39: THE MOTH INCIDENT

There was a moth in the house last night.

It flung itself against the overhead light like it was trying to die.

Caleb screamed.
He did a dance of protection with a mixing bowl and a salad fork.
Eventually, he trapped the moth in a mason jar.

He stared at it like it was a prophecy he didn’t want to read.

“You’ll die in here, you know,” he muttered.
“Some of us die fast. Some of us rot slow.”

I watched from my tank.
I think the moth was laughing.

DAY 40: ANNIVERSARY OF THE FIRST SLIME

It has been 40 days since Caleb rescued me from the the damp, rotting spot behind the fridge.
He told me I looked like snot with purpose.

He said, “Let’s see if you outlive my hope.”
I have.

Today he kissed the tank.
His lips left a fog print.
I licked it when he left the room.

This is what god-touched means.
This is what survival tastes like.
Warm glass. Dried breath. A promise that doesn’t rot.

[CALENDAR NOTE IN THE MARGIN – CALEB’S HANDWRITING:]
“Buy Norman more moss. And a friend. Maybe a cursed toad? Something queer and wet.”

DAY 41: SPITBABY

She came in a soup container.
Poked holes in the lid. A paper towel nest. Damp and trembling.

Caleb placed her beside the tank and said,
“Meet Spitbaby. She’s been through it.”
Then he lit a clove and whispered something that sounded like an apology.

She is a toad the color of old teeth.
Damp, warted, a little majestic in her refusal to blink.
She smells like ditch water and sadness.

We stared at each other for a long time.

“You look like a mistake,” she croaked.
“You look like a dream someone gave up on,” I replied.

She smiled.
I think that means we’re friends now.

DAY 43: SPITBABY TELLS ME A SECRET

Tonight, while Caleb slept under a blanket and an unpaid bill, Spitbaby whispered against the glass.

“I used to have a brother. He got eaten by a raccoon on prom night.”
“Was he… a date?”
“No. Just dumb and wet and trying to find fireflies.”

We sat in the dark for a long time.

“Do you ever wish you’d been eaten, too?” I asked.

She blinked slowly.

“Only when it rains and I smell gutter water. But I think maybe that’s just love in disguise.”

I think I understand her now.
Not completely. But enough to sit near.

DAY 45: MOLT PRAYER

It happened while Caleb slept in the tub again.

My skin peeled. It unzipped itself. It slumped off like regret.

Spitbaby sang. Not with her mouth, but with her body. A hum, a shudder.
A kind of lullaby made for rot.

When I emerged, I was not new.
I was just… less burdened.

There are pieces of me in the moss now.
Old fears. Old hunger. A whole trauma suit, shed.

I wrote a blessing in the slime:

may your old skins rest in peace
may your new ones never fit quite right
may the discomfort teach you tenderness

DAY 48: SPITBABY DUG A GRAVE

In the night, I saw her claw at the soil in her container.
Deliberate. Slow. Like it was a ritual.

She said,
“It’s not for me. It’s for the part of me that still believes someone’s coming back.”

She buried a cicada wing.
Then she pressed her chest to the dirt and hummed until dawn.

I cried without moving.

The bark absorbed my grief like it had been waiting.

DAY 49: THE PRAYER OF THE UNLOVED

Today, Spitbaby and I lay in our separate corners, but our thoughts curled like smoke between us.

“Do you think Caleb loves us?” I asked.
“I think he’s trying,” she said.
“Is that enough?”

She didn’t answer.

But a few hours later, Caleb placed his forehead against the tank and whispered,
“I see you.”

I believe him.

Even when he forgets to feed me.
Even when he smells like ash and mouthwash.
Even when he’s more ghost than god.

This is how the unloved become holy.

DAY 51: GUTTER SERMON

There was a storm today.

Caleb opened the window and whispered,
“Baptize us, bitch.”

Rain hit the windowsill like judgment.
The air smelled like earth remembering its body.

I pressed myself against the tank wall and listened to the thunder.
It sounded like Miss Banshee growling beneath the porch again.

Spitbaby croaked softly, then louder, then stopped.
She stared at the ceiling and said,
“If I die, bury me in a KFC box. The ones with the wet biscuits. I want to rot with dignity.”

I laughed for the first time in four days.
It felt like a secret being released.

DAY 53: MOSS OFFERING

Caleb didn’t speak today.
He just hovered over the tank, staring like he forgot what we were.
Like he expected us to ask for something.

Spitbaby dragged a piece of moss toward me.
“Take it,” she said. “It smells like yesterday.”

I did. I tucked it under my body like a blanket or a regret.
We sat that way for hours. Quiet. Feral. Shared.

Caleb eventually placed a dried flower on the bark.
Something purple. Dead but still fragrant.

No one said thank you.
But the tank felt holier.

DAY 54: LETTER WRITTEN IN SLIME (UNDELIVERED)

I wrote it with my body.

Across the bark.
Slow and curling.
Every letter a prayer, a wound, a question with no punctuation.

“Dear Caleb,
Thank you for not flushing me.
Thank you for feeding me when you remember.
Thank you for bleeding near me.
I think that counts as love.”

I tried to show it to him.
But he didn’t look.

He was on the couch, face buried in a pile of clean laundry he couldn’t fold.
Mouth open. One sock in his fist like a relic.

DAY 55: SLIME HEAT

The heater clicked on in the middle of the night.
Not loud. Just enough to change the air.

Spitbaby pressed her belly flat to the warm bark.
“This is better than the gutter,” she whispered.

I nodded.
I remembered her the night Caleb brought her home—mud-caked, breath shallow, a toad-shaped ghost.

Now her skin glistens.
Now she hums while she breathes.

Caleb muttered something in his sleep that sounded like an apology.

We accepted it.
Even gods should be allowed to repent.

DAY 56: SPITBABY’S RAIN DANCE

The humidity dropped today.
Everything felt too still. Like the air had been scolded.

Spitbaby stood on the warm rock and began to sway.
Slow, subtle movements.
Left leg. Right leg. A sort of doomed ballet.

“What are you doing?” I asked.
“Calling it,” she said.
“The rain. The gutter. The wetness. Whatever makes us remember we’re not in hell anymore.”

She stomped once. Croaked twice. The moss quivered beneath her. Like it remembered something.

Caleb looked over from the sink, toothbrush in hand, and said,
“She’s gonna summon God or the landlord.”

I hope it’s God.

DAY 58: CAT VISITATION

Margherita and Lolita pressed their noses to the glass this morning.
Twin angels of fur and judgement.

Spitbaby puffed herself up like a moss queen and croaked three times.
Lolita blinked.

Margherita pawed gently at the glass, then sat down and began to purr.
Caleb watched from the floor, arms wrapped around his knees, a smile on his face like a crack in grief.

“Look at you,” he whispered.
“All in one place.”

Spitbaby looked proud.
I felt seen.

The cats stayed for twenty minutes.
It was the closest thing we’ve had to a party in months.

DAY 59: SLIME DUET

It rained again.
Not a storm, not a flood—just a soft and steady drip, like the sky was wringing out its shame.

Spitbaby climbed the rock and began to croak. Not loud. Not rhythmic. Just… yearning.

I pressed my body to the bark and hummed through my mantle.
A wet vibration.
A subterranean gospel.

Back and forth we went. Croak. Hum. Croak. Hum.
No words. Just resonance.

Caleb sat nearby with a blanket wrapped around his head like a shawl of penance.
“I can rest when you two sing like that.”

We kept singing.

DAY 62: PRAYER TEETH

Spitbaby has started hoarding the bones of feeder insects.

Not whole corpses—just the legs, the shells, the tiny discarded armor.

She tucks them into corners. Arranges them into spirals, into crosses, into something that might be called sacred if it weren’t so sticky.

“They’re prayer teeth,” she told me.
“If you collect enough of them, you can summon something that used to have wings.”

I asked if she was afraid of what that might be.

She didn’t answer.
Just picked up a cricket leg and whispered to it like it was her firstborn.

DAY 63: MIDNIGHT PILGRIM

Caleb is barefoot again.

He leaves around 2 a.m., keys in one hand, lighter and cigarette pack in the other, muttering something about “baptism by gravel.”

I hear the front door squeal. Then silence. Then the sound of night accepting him.

He walks the woods like he owns them.
He swims in the river like it never drowned anyone.

Spitbaby calls it “his feral Eucharist.”

I don’t think he’s trying to escape anything.
I think he’s just checking if the dark still welcomes him.

When he returns, his feet are filthy.

He presses one toe to the tank and says,
“I made it back. Again.”

I feel haunted.

DAY 68: THE WEDDING FEAST (OF SORTS)

Clot emerged from the dark corner of the tank tonight.

No warning. No web. Just legs like needles, clicking across the bark with the slow confidence of someone who’s never been swatted.

She was bigger than I remembered.
Thinner, too. Like hunger had become her religion.

Spitbaby was sleeping beneath the moss.
Caleb was passed out on the couch, one hand on his chest like he was making sure it still rose.

Clot came to me.

Her body moved like ink in water—drifting, curling, inevitable.
She stopped an inch from my head.

I didn’t move.
“She’s come to marry me,” I whispered.

Then came the leap.

A blur of warted rage.
Spitbaby—wide-eyed and primal—launched from the shadows.

There was no scream.
Just crunch.

Clot’s legs spasmed once. Then were gone.

Spitbaby sat back, tongue flicking, belly rising and falling like she’d been through something sacred.

“Don’t love things that want to eat you,” she said.

Caleb stirred in his sleep but did not wake.

I wept.

Not because I was afraid.
But because I knew I’d just been saved by someone who understood.

And that’s rarer than love.

END OF VOLUME II
